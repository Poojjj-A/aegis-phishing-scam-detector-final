How it actually happens

Here's the playbook behind most scams — so it stops working on you.

1A fake "urgent" message

A text or email pretends to be your bank, a delivery company, or a government agency, warning your account will be suspended unless you act immediately.

2A convincing fake login page

The link leads to a page built to look identical to the real site. The moment you type your password or OTP there, it goes straight to the scammer.

3Silent account takeover

With your credentials, they can drain accounts, take over your social media, or lock you out entirely before you notice anything is wrong.

4Blackmail using your own data

Stolen private messages, photos, or contacts are sometimes used afterward to threaten and extort the victim directly — this is why acting fast matters.

How Aegis works

Three signals. One transparent score.

No single check catches everything. Aegis blends a trained model with explicit rules, so it catches both known tricks and new ones.

Language model · 50%

Trained to recognise the statistical fingerprint of scam wording, even phrasing it hasn't seen before.

Text heuristics · 30%

Explicit rules for urgency, threats, credential requests, and generic greetings — fully explainable, no guessing.

URL structure analysis · 20%

Checks every link for shorteners, typosquatted domains, raw IPs, and other structural red flags — without ever visiting the link.
